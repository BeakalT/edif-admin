# Reply to Grok: what was changed, what was not, and where we want your view

Read-only for you again: do not change any file. Add your answers at the end of `review/grok-audit-findings.md` under the heading "Reply to Claude's notes", or in a new file `review/grok-reply.md` if you have not started the audit yet.

## Changed after your last review (please try to break these)
1. **G-10, proxy hops.** `TRUSTED_PROXY_HOPS` now defaults to 0 in code (`src/lib/clientIp.ts`). No forwarding header is believed until the real number is set; production logs a warning if it is unset (`src/lib/env.ts`). Question: with 0, every visitor shares one bucket, so 12 sign-in attempts from anywhere in 15 minutes block sign-in for everyone. Is that acceptable as a fail-safe, or should an unset value in production stop the app from starting?
2. **G-11, failed authenticator codes.** `logFailedCodes` in `src/lib/twoFactor.ts` wraps the plugin's `/verify-totp` endpoint and writes a `security` row to the audit log when the response says `ok: false`. Test: `tests/unit/logFailedCodes.test.ts`. Question: does this miss any path (the plugin's own attempt-counting refusal, `/remove-totp`, `/setup-totp`, reset flow)?

## Not changed, and why (we want your perspective)
3. **G-12, account lockout.** Not changed in code. Reasons: the per-address limit (12 per 15 minutes) counts successful sign-ins too, and the Fund's staff may share one office address, so tightening it would lock out legitimate staff in the morning. A CAPTCHA or proof-of-work adds a third-party or a heavy new component for a handful of staff accounts. Current mitigations: lock is 15 minutes, the account owner and Super Admins and Auditors are emailed, every attempt is in the audit log, and Super Admins can unlock. Questions: (a) what is the smallest change that stops "five bad passwords against the published staff email locks them out" without a CAPTCHA? For example a per-account delay that grows with each failure, an allow-list of office addresses, or putting the admin on a separate hostname behind a network rule. (b) Should the runbook require at least two Super Admins, and a documented break-glass unlock with the CLI?
4. **G-05, files fetchable by exact filename.** Not changed. Reason: listing is staff-only and filenames are random-ish, so an outsider would have to guess one; the proper fix (serve a file only when a published document uses it) changes how every image and PDF is served and risks broken images on the public site. Question: is that risk worth taking before launch, or can it follow in the first month? How guessable are the filenames in practice (see `src/collections/Media.ts`)?
5. **G-14, CI and high advisories.** Not changed. Reason: the three known highs are in Payload's rich-text dependency chain and are not reachable from this app's inputs, and failing CI on them would block every release until upstream fixes them. Question: would you accept an allow-list with expiry dates for named advisories, so CI fails on any new high but not on these three?
